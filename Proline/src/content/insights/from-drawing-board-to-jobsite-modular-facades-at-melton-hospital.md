The first two modular facade panels are now installed at Melton Hospital.

It is a small number compared with the approximately 1,000 panels still to follow, but it represents a significant milestone for Proline. The installation is the result of more than 18 months spent drawing, designing, testing and learning how to make modular facades work at genuine project scale.

Working with Lendlease, our team has developed a system designed to meet four clear requirements. It needs to make financial sense, perform structurally, remain watertight and move efficiently through manufacturing and installation.

Seeing the first panels standing on site is where that work becomes real.

## Why Modular Facades Matter

Large projects create a difficult delivery challenge. Quality needs to remain consistent across hundreds of repeated elements, while manufacturing, transport and installation must continue to move at pace.

A modular facade approach shifts a greater share of that work into a controlled factory environment. Panels can be assembled, checked and prepared before they reach site, reducing the number of separate activities that need to happen at height or within a congested work area.

The value is not simply speed. A successful modular system needs to connect design, engineering, waterproofing, manufacturing, logistics and installation. Every interface has to be considered before production begins because repetition magnifies both good decisions and poor ones.

That is why the work behind the first panel matters so much.

## Eighteen Months of Development

For the past 18 months, Proline has invested thousands of hours into understanding the system as a complete delivery process.

Our design and detailing teams have worked through panel geometry, structural requirements, interfaces and tolerances. Testing has focused on how the system performs, while factory planning has considered how panels move from materials through fabrication, assembly, quality assurance and dispatch.

At the same time, the installation methodology has been developed around the realities of a major live construction site. A system can work perfectly in a model and still fail if it is difficult to manufacture, transport or install. Each stage has to support the next.

This is where Proline's integrated model becomes valuable. Design, fabrication and installation sit within one business, allowing information to move directly between the people developing the system, building the panels and installing them on site.

## From the Factory to the Jobsite

The first installation is an important validation point. It allows the team to see the complete process operating under real site conditions and confirm that the work completed in design and manufacturing translates into an efficient installation.

It also creates a practical feedback loop. Lessons from site can return quickly to the factory and design teams, helping refine the process as production scales.

## Built for Scale

Melton Hospital is a substantial project, and the aerial imagery makes that scale immediately clear. This is not a small trial or a standalone prototype. The first two panels are the beginning of a program that will involve approximately 1,000 modular facade panels.

Delivering at that scale requires more than production capacity. It needs repeatable systems, clear quality controls, coordinated logistics and teams that understand how their work affects every stage that follows.

For Proline, the project represents the next step in a capability we have deliberately built over time. Our investment in design, technology, local fabrication and experienced site teams allows us to take greater ownership of complex facade packages from concept through to installation.

## The First Two of Approximately 1,000

There is a long way to go at Melton Hospital, and the project remains firmly in progress. That makes this milestone more meaningful, not less.

The first panels demonstrate that the system has moved beyond drawings and testing. It is now part of a live delivery program on one of Victoria's largest hospital projects.

Modular construction will play an increasingly important role in how major projects are delivered. Proline intends to help lead that shift by building systems that are practical, tested and backed by the people who will manufacture and install them.

The first two panels are up. Now the real scale of the work begins.
